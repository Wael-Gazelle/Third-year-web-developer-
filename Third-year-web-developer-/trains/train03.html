Once upon a time in spring, at the hour of an unusually hot sunset, in Moscow, at the Patriarch's Ponds, two citizens appeared. The first of them, dressed in a light grey summer suit , was short in stature, well-fed, bald, carrying his decent hat in his hand, and on his well-shaven face were supernatural glasses in black horn-rimmed frames. The second was a broad-shouldered, reddish-haired, curly-haired young man in a checked cap tilted back on his head, was wearing a cowboy hat, chewed white trousers and black slippers.
The first was none other than Mikhail Alexandrovich Berlioz, chairman of the board of one of the largest Moscow literary associations, abbreviated as MASSOLIT, and editor of a thick art magazine, and his young companion, the poet Ivan Nikolaevich Ponyrev, writing under the pseudonym Homeless
Finding themselves in the shade of slightly green lime trees, the writers rushed to a colourfully painted booth with the inscription ‘Beer and Water’
